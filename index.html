The evidence

Look around, mi amor. Where's the curve?

Other chapters need telescopes and laser rigs. We have the 305. Every day in Miami is a field study in flatness, and the results have been consistent since Flagler.

01

We have never seen a hill

The only incline in the county is the Rickenbacker Causeway, which the government built. A man-made hill is a confession.

02

Humidity, not gravity

Step outside in August. You are not being pulled toward a spinning ball. You are being pressed flat by the air itself. It's obvious.

03

The water stays put

If the Earth were round, the ocean would roll off. Instead it comes up through the storm drains on a sunny Tuesday and parks on Alton Road.

04

Hurricanes spin

They spin because the Earth is a big flat table and the sky has a ceiling fan. The cone of uncertainty is the only round thing we accept.

05

The sunset

The sun goes down into the Everglades every night. Have you been out on Tamiami Trail after dark? Exactly. Nobody checks.

06

Ships vanish hull-first

Round-earthers say curvature. We say the ships are stuck behind a cruise terminal waiting on the Port of Miami tunnel.

Official cartography

Miami is the center of the disc.

Everybody already knew this. The rest of the world simply orbits around us, culturally and literally. Our chapter cartographer drew this on the back of a pastelito bag and it has held up under peer review.

  • Miami. The center. Confirmed.
  • Florida and the Caribbean. The inner ring. Family lives here.
  • Everywhere else. Includes New York, which keeps sending people down here anyway.
  • The Ice Wall. The outer edge, and the origin of every snowbird. They arrive each November with sunburn and a Buick. Draw your own conclusions.

Preguntas frecuentes

Questions from your cousin who "did some reading."

What about the photos from space?

Shot in a warehouse in Doral, between a freight forwarder and a gym that only plays reggaetón. The lighting is great in there.

Why do planes take longer to fly to Europe than to Bogotá?

Customs at MIA. That's it. That's the curvature people are feeling.

Is the Bermuda Triangle real?

It's the drain. Every flat surface needs one. Please stop asking us to go look at it.

Why can't I see Cuba from the Southernmost Point buoy?

Humidity. On a dry day you could see straight to Havana, but Key West has never had a dry day.

Do you accept round things?

Croquetas are cylinders, so they're fine. Pastelitos are flat and therefore sacred. Our treasurer keeps the dues in a Pringles can, which is under review by the ethics committee.

Is the sea level really rising?

Yes, and this is the one part we're dead serious about. Even on a flat earth, Miami-Dade sits a few feet above high tide. Go read the county's resilience plan. Then come to the meeting.
